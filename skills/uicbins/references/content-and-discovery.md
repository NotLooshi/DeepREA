# Content and discovery

Use for public business, product or informational pages, substantial content redesigns, or explicit SEO work. This adds a content contract to the existing blueprint, not a separate product or compulsory marketing funnel. Private apps need clear labels and truthful information; they do not automatically need acquisition copy, a sitemap or merchant markup. Classify **public page / private app / demo** and intended discovery before applying checks. A public-page prototype can still request metadata checks without pretending it is a live business.

## Start from the reader's decision

Before choosing composition, record the audience, their question, the actual offer/entity and intended next action. Start from supplied specifications, actual product/service evidence, customer/support questions and authorised search data when available. Label inferred search intent; do not invent search volumes, customer research or ranking forecasts.

Make a short content map: **reader question → sourced answer → useful proof → next action**. Record missing decision-relevant facts as owner questions, then continue independent work. For a product these might include dimensions, compatibility, brightness, replacement parts or warranty terms. For a service they might include scope, eligibility, location, price basis and next-step expectations. These are conditional questions, not universal mandatory sections. Never invent missing answers, testimonials, stock, service conditions or FAQs to fill a layout.

Each substantial section should help identify, explain, compare, decide or act. A prominent image should explain the subject or support a claim. Captioning an unrelated image honestly does not make it useful. Omit unused template sections. Do not add photographs, videos, headings or copy merely to satisfy an aesthetic quota. Preserve useful creative voice while making the entity and distinguishing facts intelligible.

## Keep the offer and action coherent

Check headline, descriptive copy, pricing/availability, action label, form and confirmation as one promise. A waitlist CTA implies a different commitment from an available product or a local demo. If the form only records interest locally, say so; do not imply delivery of an email, a purchase or a server-side booking. Do not invent an availability change to explain the CTA. Guide discoverable cost information through the existing pricing policy; deliberate omission remains possible.

Review content against its source ledger, not just against other sentences on the page. Consistent invented facts are still invented. Separate factual contradictions, missing supplied facts, missing owner information and stylistic preferences. For material claims, retain a short source pointer; a schema or model score is not proof.

## Public-page foundations

- Use a descriptive page title and intelligible main heading identifying the actual subject. Creative headings can work with a clear product/category label. No fixed heading formula or arbitrary character quota.
- Write a specific, truthful meta description where public discovery is intended. Google may choose another snippet; presence alone does not establish ranking or clicks.
- Use real `a href` destinations for navigation and native buttons for actions. Test fragment targets. A same-page fragment does not create another indexable page.
- Keep important content available in the delivered HTML or a verified crawler-compatible render. For static public pages, make essential content visible by default. Add motion after successful initialization; an animation/script failure must not leave core answers at opacity zero. Test the no-script state with the supplied essential selectors, and test reduced motion separately.
- Use accurate image descriptions where images convey information, and empty alternatives for decoration where appropriate. Verify subject relevance, intrinsic dimensions and loading strategy. Do not stuff keywords into descriptions or substitute unrelated photos for product evidence.

Run [discovery checking](discovery-checking.md) for the applicable metadata, fragment and selected essential-content contract. It does not judge semantic truth, visual relevance or actual search performance. Pair it with the content map and rendered review. Keep mechanical discovery findings separate from editorial findings.

## Conditional launch checks

Only add a canonical when the real preferred public URL and duplication context are known. Do not manufacture a production domain for a local demo. Structured data must describe real applicable entities and visible supported facts, and follow the specific feature rules. Never invent ratings, reviews, prices, stock or business identity. A fictional non-purchasable demonstration is not eligible for merchant listings merely because it depicts a product. Validation of syntax is separate from eligibility and truth.

On an authorised real deployment, verify HTTP status, redirect behavior, robots/header directives, canonical destinations, meaningful internal links and intended indexing. Use access control for private content; robots/noindex is not confidentiality. Do not automatically noindex public deliverables. If a public preview should be excluded, confirm its indexing intent and verify the directive can be read.

Label lab performance as lab evidence. Live indexing, Search Console data, field Core Web Vitals and search/click/conversion outcomes require their own measurements. Local files, screenshots and check counts cannot establish them. Keep unavailable deployment checks explicitly unverified; do not turn them into fabricated failures or passes.

### Audit added promises too

After composing copy, compare new operational claims back to the supplied facts, in addition to checking that facts are present. Preserve uncertainty and conditions: an estimated turnaround is not a guaranteed completion date or an instruction to collect after that interval. Do not invent a readiness notification to repair the wording. Rewrite unsupported commitments using the supplied estimate/condition or surface the missing owner decision. Mark this as content review, not a metadata or string-presence pass.

## Final published-claim audit

Audit the actual final headings, descriptions, image captions, CTA labels, metadata and confirmation copy, not only the supplied-fact checklist. For each decision-relevant factual promise, record a compact entry: exact published wording/location → supplied source passage → supported, qualified, or removed. Group repeated equivalent claims. Keep this in the existing QA record; do not demand a row for every adjective.

Include take-home deliverables, quantities, technique details, duration, eligibility, location, availability and what happens after the action. A workshop title alone does not establish what participants produce or take away; an evocative service name does not establish a start time, season or day-to-night schedule. Short card labels and atmospheric supporting copy can imply operational facts too. A fictional business label does not authorize inventing specific service promises unless the user explicitly requested invented scenario facts. Mark proposed facts as proposals in the review record, not as established customer-facing copy.

For example, a brief supplying only a print-workshop name, price and duration does not support “Leave with a stack” or “Make a two-colour print”. Use the known workshop name/process, price and duration; omit the deliverable or ask the owner if it materially changes the offer. If the brief explicitly supplies a two-colour take-home print, keep that useful specificity. “Make your mark” may be creative metaphor when its context does not imply a guaranteed deliverable. Do not flatten all expressive language into generic factual prose.

Have the adversarial reviewer inspect additions against the brief, including implications across headline and imagery. Missing information is not permission to fill it with a plausible promise. After copy repairs, repeat the audit on changed sentences and any dependent CTA/metadata. String-presence, metadata and geometry checks cannot certify this semantic audit.
